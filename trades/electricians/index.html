How people search for an electrician

There are three main kinds of electrical search, and most electricians' websites only really cater for one of them.

Faults and emergencies

A fuse board that keeps tripping, half the house without power, a burning smell from a socket. These searches are urgent, made on a phone, and usually go to whoever is in the top three on Google Maps with a number that dials straight away.

Certificates and compliance

Electrical installation condition reports (EICRs) for landlords, checks before a house sale, and sign-off for new work. Landlords and letting agents want a fixed price, a quick date and the report sent over without chasing.

Upgrades and projects

Consumer unit replacements, full and partial rewires, extra sockets, garden lighting, EV chargers, solar panels and battery storage. People research these, compare a few firms, and choose on photos, reviews and qualifications.

Getting into the map pack

When someone searches "electrician near me", Google shows three businesses on a map before any websites. Being one of them comes down to your Google Business Profile, how close you are to the searcher and your reviews. The first and last you can control.

  • Electrician as your main category. It sounds obvious, but I often see electricians listed under a general contractor category, or with the categories left on whatever was picked at sign-up.
  • Your real services listed. EICRs, consumer unit replacement, rewiring, EV charger installation, fault finding. Each is a search Google can match you to.
  • Photos that show your standard. A neat, labelled consumer unit says more about your work than anything you can write. So does a tidy EV charger on a customer's drive.
  • Reviews that mention the job and place. "Replaced our fuse board in Wimborne, really tidy job" gives Google the job and the town. Ask for that detail when you send the review link.
  • Matching details everywhere. Your NICEIC or NAPIT listing, Checkatrade and Facebook page should all use the same business name and number as your Google profile.

Landlord and letting-agent work

Private landlords in England have to have the electrics in their rental properties inspected and tested at least every five years, and give the report to tenants. That makes condition reports steady, repeat work. It's also the reason letting agents keep a short list of electricians they trust.

Agents usually find you once, then search your business name every time after that. So your Google profile needs to look active, with recent reviews and accurate hours. Your website needs a landlord page that answers their questions before they ask.

A landlord page should cover

  • What an EICR includes and roughly how long it takes
  • How quickly you can usually book one in
  • How remedial work is quoted if something fails
  • How the report is sent, and to whom
  • Whether you work directly with letting agents

Showing NICEIC or NAPIT properly

Being registered with a competent person scheme such as NICEIC or NAPIT lets you self-certify notifiable work, and it's one of the first things a careful customer checks. Most electricians put a logo in the footer and leave it there. A few small changes do more:

  • Link the logo to your own entry on the scheme's register, so customers can check it in one tap.
  • Mention the scheme in your page titles or opening lines where it fits, such as "NICEIC-registered electrician in Poole".
  • Keep the business name on the register identical to your Google profile. The register is itself a listing Google can see.
  • If you install EV chargers or solar, show the certifications those jobs need on those pages, not just on the home page.

Electrician SEO questions

Is it worth having a separate page for EICRs?

Yes, if you want landlord and house-sale work. People searching for an electrical condition report want to know the price range, how quickly you can do it and what happens if something fails. A general electrical services page can't answer that, and it won't rank for it either.

Can I put my NICEIC or NAPIT membership on my Google profile?

There isn't a box for it, but you can mention it in your business description and add a photo of your certificate or van livery. The bigger win is making sure your business name, number and address on the scheme's own find-a-contractor listing match your Google profile exactly.

Should I go after EV charger and solar work through SEO?

If you're qualified for it and want it, yes. These are planned, higher-value jobs, and customers research them properly before choosing. A dedicated page with photos of your installs, the certifications you hold and what the job involves gives you a real chance against the national installers.

I'm busy with word of mouth. Why would I need SEO?

Word of mouth is great, but you don't choose what it sends you. SEO lets you aim for the work you'd rather do, such as rewires and consumer units instead of single sockets. And when someone recommends you, the first thing they do is search your name, so your Google profile still matters.
