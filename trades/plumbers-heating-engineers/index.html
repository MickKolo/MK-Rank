How people search for a plumber or heating engineer

The words people type, and the way they choose, change with how urgent the job is. Your Google profile and website need to work for each kind of customer.

"I need someone now"

A burst pipe, a leak through the ceiling, no hot water, a boiler showing a fault code. These searches happen on a phone, often with "near me" or "emergency" in them. People call one of the first three map results and rarely scroll further.

What wins: a place in the map pack, a number that dials when tapped, and reviews that mention turning up quickly.

"I'm planning a job"

A boiler replacement, a new bathroom, a cylinder swap or underfloor heating. People research these over days, usually on a laptop, and look at photos, reviews and accreditations before asking two or three firms to quote.

What wins: a proper page for each job type, photos of finished work, and a clear idea of what's included.

"I look after property"

Landlords and letting agents need gas safety checks, boiler services and repairs, often for several properties at once. They want quick quotes, paperwork sent over without chasing, and one firm they can rely on.

What wins: a landlord page that explains how you work, and being easy to find by your business name.

Getting into the map pack

For most plumbing and heating searches, the three Google Maps results at the top take most of the calls. Your Google Business Profile decides whether you're one of them, and most of the profiles I look at are missing easy wins.

  • The right categories. A primary category that matches the work you want most, such as Plumber or Heating contractor, with the rest added as secondary categories.
  • Every service listed. Boiler installation, boiler servicing, leak repairs, bathroom fitting, landlord gas safety checks. Google matches searches against these.
  • A service area you really cover. If you won't drive to Bridport for a dripping tap, don't list it. Customers you can't serve cost you reviews and time.
  • Photos of real jobs. A neat boiler install with tidy pipework, or a bathroom before and after. Customers judge your standard of work from them, and stock photos give nothing away.
  • Reviews that describe the job. "Fixed our leaking cylinder in Broadstone the same day" helps far more than "great service". A short text after each job, with your review link, is enough to ask for it.
  • Hours that match your phone. Set them to when you really answer, including bank holidays, so you show as open when it counts.

The heating side: boilers, gas and the winter rush

Heating searches follow the weather. Breakdown calls jump with the first cold snap, while boiler replacements are often researched in the quieter months. Your website should be ready before the rush, not during it.

Show your Gas Safe registration

Gas work in homes and most workplaces has to be done by a Gas Safe registered engineer, and customers are told to check before they let anyone start. Put your registration number on your website and in your Google profile, and link to your entry on the register so it takes one tap to confirm.

Use your manufacturer approvals

If you're an accredited installer for a boiler brand, people search for that by name, such as "Worcester Bosch installer Poole", because it can mean a longer guarantee. Give it a line on your boiler page and a mention in your Google profile.

Separate breakdowns from installs

"Boiler repair" and "new boiler" are different searches from different customers. One page trying to cover both usually ranks for neither. Give each its own page, with servicing either on the repairs page or on its own.

Cover what's off the gas network

Plenty of rural Dorset homes run on oil or LPG, and some are moving to heat pumps. If you do that work, say so with its own page and the right certification, such as OFTEC for oil or MCS for heat pumps. Few local firms do, so it's easier to rank for.

Pages a plumbing and heating website needs

Most plumbing websites I check have one services page listing twenty jobs in bullet points. Google can't rank a bullet point for "boiler installation Poole", so the work you want most never gets found.

  1. One page for each main job you want more of. Boiler installs, repairs and servicing, bathrooms, emergency call-outs, landlord work. Each one says what's included, roughly how long it takes, and shows photos from real jobs.
  2. A page for each main area, written about that area. The housing, the travel time and the jobs you do there, not the same page with the town name swapped.
  3. A number that works. A tap-to-call number at the top of every page. On a surprising number of trade sites I've tested, tapping the number doesn't actually call.
  4. A real about page. Your face, your van, your Gas Safe number and how long you've been trading. People are letting you into their home.

Plumbing and heating SEO questions

Can I rank for emergency call-outs if I don't work 24 hours?

Yes. Set your Google opening hours to when you really answer the phone, and you can still win urgent searches during those hours. Listing 24/7 when you don't pick up at night leads to missed calls and bad reviews, which costs you more than it gains.

Should I keep paying for Checkatrade or MyBuilder?

Keep them while they pay for themselves, but treat them as a top-up, not the plan. On lead sites you're often one of several firms sent the same job, and the customer belongs to the platform. A customer who finds you on Google calls you, and comes back to you when the boiler needs its next service. Use the same business name, number and service list on both so each backs the other up.

Do I need a separate page for every town I cover?

No. A page for each main area you want work in is worth having, but only if it says something real about that area. Twenty pages with the town name swapped are what Google calls doorway pages, and they can drag the whole site down. Most plumbing and heating firms do better with strong pages for their main jobs and a handful of genuine area pages.

Is SEO worth it for a one-van plumber?

Usually, yes, as long as you have room for more work. A single boiler install or bathroom job is normally worth more than the whole fix-up, and the profile and pages keep working after that. If you're already turning work away, the better goal is more of the jobs you prefer, not more calls.
