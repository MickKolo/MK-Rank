How people search for a roofer

The urgency of the problem changes how people search and how they choose. Your Google profile and website need to work for both.

Leaks and storm damage

Water through the ceiling, slipped tiles, a flat roof that has started to pool. These searches spike after bad weather and happen on a phone. People call whoever shows up first with good reviews and a number that dials.

Planned roofing

Re-roofs, flat roof replacements, fascias, soffits and guttering, chimney repairs and roof windows. People research these over weeks, get several quotes and compare photos, guarantees and reviews.

Landlords and insurance work

Landlords and letting agents want repairs sorted quickly with photos to prove it. Insurance claims need clear photos and a written report, and customers search for roofers who can help with both.

Getting into the map pack

For "roofer near me" and most roofing searches, Google shows three businesses on a map before any websites. Your Google Business Profile decides whether you're one of them.

  • The right category and services. Roofing contractor as your main category, with your services listed: roof repairs, re-roofing, flat roofs, guttering, chimney work. Google matches searches against them.
  • Photos from up on the roof. Before and after shots of real jobs show customers work they could never see from the ground. They're your best sales tool.
  • Reviews that mention the job and place. "Replaced our flat roof in Parkstone, left the site spotless" gives Google the service and the area.
  • Quick replies after a storm. Answer calls and messages fast, keep your hours accurate, and post an update on your profile when you're taking storm-damage calls.
  • A service area you really cover. That way the calls you get are for jobs you'll actually take on.

Storm damage and seasonal demand

Dorset's coast takes the full force of winter gales, and searches for roof repairs jump after every big storm. The roofers who get those calls are the ones who already have a storm-damage page live and a Google profile that shows they're open and answering.

The rest of the year is when planned work gets researched and booked. Use the quieter months to write up recent re-roofs and flat roof jobs, so they're ranking when people start planning for the next summer.

A storm-damage page should cover

  • What you can do straight away to make the roof safe
  • How quickly you can usually get out
  • How you help with photos and reports for insurance
  • What the permanent repair involves

Trust: the part of the job customers can't see

Most homeowners will never climb up to check your work, and many have heard stories about doorstep roofers. That makes proof more important for roofers than for almost any other trade.

  • Memberships, linked. If you're a member of the National Federation of Roofing Contractors, registered with CompetentRoofer or TrustMark, show the logos and link them to your listings so customers can check.
  • Photos before and after. Show the damage you found and the finished job on every project write-up.
  • Guarantees in writing. Say how long your workmanship guarantee lasts and what it covers, and mention any manufacturer warranties on materials.
  • Insurance. Say plainly that you carry public liability insurance.
  • A real face behind the business. Your name, your van and how long you've been trading. Customers want to know who they're dealing with.

Roofer SEO questions

Roofers have a bad name thanks to doorstep traders. How do I stand out?

Show the work customers can't see for themselves. Photos of the problem before you start and the finished roof afterwards, your memberships linked to their listings, written guarantees and reviews that describe the job. A customer comparing you with a stranger at the door will pick the one with the proof.

Should I have a page for storm damage?

Yes, and get it live before the winter rather than after the first storm. Explain what you can do straight away to make a roof safe, what happens next and how you help with insurance photos. When the wind picks up, that page and your Google profile are what people find.

I do flat roofs and pitched roofs. Do I need separate pages?

Yes. People search for them differently, such as "flat roof replacement" or "EPDM roof" against "re-roof" or "roof tiles", and the customers often have different jobs in mind. One page trying to cover both usually ranks for neither.

I'm booked up for months. Why would I need SEO?

Being booked up is a good position to choose your work from. SEO can bring in more of the jobs you prefer, such as full re-roofs rather than small repairs, and it keeps enquiries coming in when the busy season ends.
