Ranking a trades business in Poole

Poole wraps around one of the largest natural harbours in the world. That geography shapes local search more than most trades realise. The town is a string of distinct neighbourhoods around the water, from Hamworthy and the quay to Parkstone, Lilliput and Sandbanks, with Broadstone, Creekmoor, Canford Heath and Oakdale inland.

Google shows map results based partly on how close you are to the searcher, so a firm based in Broadstone and a firm based in Hamworthy can see very different results for the same search. Customers also search by neighbourhood, especially in the more affluent areas, where "builder Lilliput" or "electrician Canford Cliffs" is a realistic search.

Three very different kinds of Poole customer

The peninsulas and the harbour edge

Sandbanks has some of the most expensive property in the country. Customers there, and in nearby Lilliput and Canford Cliffs, commission larger projects, compare several firms and look closely at your website and photos before they call. Coastal homes also take a battering from salt air and wind, so external work comes up often.

The established suburbs

Parkstone, Broadstone, Oakdale, Creekmoor and Canford Heath make up most of Poole's homes, and most of its everyday searches: boiler breakdowns, leaks, consumer units and extensions. Speed, reviews and being in the map pack decide who gets the call.

Landlords and businesses

Student lets around Talbot Village, where an Article 4 Direction on HMOs has applied since May 2013, and the businesses around the port and the boatyards generate steady compliance and commercial work. These customers search by service, not by neighbourhood.

Old Town, the quay and period property

Poole's Old Town has been a conservation area since 1975. Its streets hold Georgian merchants' houses built on 18th-century trade with North America, as well as listed buildings such as the Grade II* Customs House on the quay, built in 1814. Work on listed buildings can need listed building consent, and owners look for trades who have done that kind of job before.

If you have that experience, say so plainly on your website: the type of property, the materials and what you did. It's exactly the detail someone searching for a sympathetic repair is looking for, and it's rare on trades websites.

Getting found across the harbour and the bridges

  • Set your service area properly. Make sure you're eligible to appear across the whole of Poole, not just around your base.
  • Give Hamworthy its own mention. It's across the water from the town centre, and customers there often think of it as a separate place.
  • Target the jobs you want. Separate pages for kitchen fitting, rewires, boiler installs or extensions bring better enquiries than one generic services page.
  • Show local proof. Photos of real Poole jobs, and reviews that mention the area, build trust with customers and with Google.

Poole local SEO questions

Is it worth targeting Sandbanks and Lilliput specifically?

If you do higher-value work such as renovations, kitchens, bathrooms or full rewires, yes. These are small, well-defined areas, so one strong page with photos of comparable projects can rank well. If most of your work is quick call-outs, a broader Poole page usually serves you better.

I do commercial and marine work. Can local SEO help with that?

Yes, but it works differently. Business customers search for the service itself, such as commercial electrician or industrial plumbing, and often check your website before they call. A dedicated commercial page, the right secondary categories on your Google profile and examples of past site work matter more than review volume.

Should my Google listing say Poole or Bournemouth?

Use the town where your business is actually based, then list the other towns you cover in your service area. Poole, Bournemouth and Christchurch share the BCP Council area, but Google treats them as separate places, and customers search for them separately.
